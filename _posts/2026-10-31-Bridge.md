Loved sci-fi since I was a kid, when I first saw [Space: 1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space:_1999). The genre has been a great inspiration throughout my life. When I saw Star Trek, I was intrigued by the cool-looking screens. Yeah, I just had to do this.

[gluon](https://github.com/isene/gluon) is where I start my day. Mail, calendar and tasks at the top, Claude in [deck](https://isene.org/2026/10/Deck.html) at the bottom. It looked like this:

![gluon in its plain look](/assets/posts/bridge-plain.png)

Note: All screenshots display dummy data.

My terminal, [glass](https://github.com/isene/glass), paints pixels straight from a program. That's how the [funkey](https://isene.org/funkey/) games run in it. So I asked Claude: can we make gluon look like LCARS, the screens on the Enterprise in Star Trek: The Next Generation? Just because.

Of course we could.

## How it works

gluon paints one picture over the whole screen. Orange arms, rounded corners, and a coloured button for each part. The picture has holes where the text goes, so the text stays text.

A new picture goes out only when something changes: I move to another box, a title changes, the window changes size. The clock and the stardate are plain text. When nothing happens, nothing new is sent but the minutes on the clock. Drawing a new picture takes about a hundredth of a second.

Here the part with the keys lights up and reaches over to touch its box. The other buttons go dark:

![The message box has the keys](/assets/posts/bridge-mail.png)

![The task list has the keys](/assets/posts/bridge-tasks.png)

`Ctrl+Shift+B` switches it on and off.
