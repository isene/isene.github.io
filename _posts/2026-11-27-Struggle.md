In my [coaching](/coaching/), I have sat with people who wanted to end their lives. None of them had a hard life. Not one said "my life is too much to bear."

Quite the opposite. Their lives were too easy. Nothing was asked of them, nothing was at stake, and they saw no reason to get up in the morning. They felt disillusioned. Empty. Purpose had left, and life went with it.

This is obviously shitty. But now it worries me in a new way.

## AI

I love coding with Claude. I ask, and code appears. It is a joy, and I use it every day.

But let's [extrapolate this](/2019/02/Exciting.html). Ask, and dinner appears in the fridge. Ask, and the house is clean, the work is done, the homework done. Everything automated, everything handled. NOTHING left that will ask anything of you. Full on [Wall-E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WALL-E).

When nothing requires effort, [purpose fades](https://isene.me/2016/03/27/the-dangers-of-comfort/). There's no reason to do anything. And with nothing to do, there is less reason to live. I know, I build these tools myself. I am not against AI. I am against removing every last bit of struggle from a human life.

## The tribe

The Western world has more depression than East Africa. I have [met tribes there](https://isene.me/2011/06/26/significant-change/) where depression is rare.

They are busy. They [guard the cattle](https://isene.me/2011/05/25/dont-touch-the-cows/). They watch for lions and for rival tribes. There is no time for depression. In the West we have plenty of time for that. And for all kinds of misery.

To be fair: depression exists in Africa too. It is underdiagnosed, and it often shows up as physical pain rather than sadness. But in my experience it really seemed less common there.

Sebastian Junger describes the same thing in his book [Tribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribe_%28Junger_book%29). People in close groups who face hardship together often do well. People in comfortable, lonely modern life often do worse. Hardship shared is a bond. Comfort alone is a trap. This is also covered in Nassim Nicholas Taleb's [Antifragile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antifragile_%28book%29).

## The why

Viktor Frankl survived the concentration camps and wrote [Man's Search for Meaning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man%27s_Search_for_Meaning). His life work, [logotherapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logotherapy), comes down to one finding: what keeps a person going is a Why. A reason. A purpose.

Take away struggle and you take away purpose. Take away purpose and there is less reason to live.

This is why I partly side with the [anti-AI crowd](/2026/01/AI-Coding.html). Claude is wonderful, and I would not give it up. But we should not go all in and automate everything. A life with zero friction is not a good life. It is the lives of the clients I told you about.

## The choice

I do not romanticise poverty, illness or danger. That is not struggle, that is suffering, and nobody should have too much of that.

The struggle I'm talking about here is chosen. A hard project. A skill you grind at for years. A fit body. A craft. Something that pushes back when you push it. Stuff that hardens you and helps make you Antifragile.

The beauty of a chosen struggle: it gives you a why without giving you a war. You pick the mountain. Climbing it is the point.

For me, the [calculator collection](/hp-41/) was never about having the collection. It was about collecting. That's why I sold it this summer, as I was pretty much done.

So keep some struggle in your life, on purpose. Let Claude write the boring code. Then go lift something heavy, learn something hard, build something that resists you. Do some hard shit.
