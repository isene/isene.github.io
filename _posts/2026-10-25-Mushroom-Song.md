![A fly agaric in an autumn birch forest, wired to a small circuit board, with music notes rising from its cap](/assets/posts/mushroom-song.jpg)

In September a colleague tagged me in our team chat:

> MIDI file from about 30 minutes of recording a living fly agaric in the forest 😂

He built the instrument himself. Two electrodes in the stem of the mushroom. A small timer chip that turns the mushroom's electrical resistance into a tone. A Raspberry Pi Pico that measures that tone 200 times a second. A laptop that turns the drift into notes in C minor pentatonic.

The mushroom is the only thing making the music. Left alone it plays some 20 notes a minute. Touch it and it plays a flurry. The idea comes from the open [Biodata Sonification](https://github.com/electricityforprogress/BiodataSonificationBreadboardKit) project, the same family as [PlantWave](https://www.plantwave.com).

Lewis is a brilliant guy in my IT OPs team. He's smart, and as you can see quite creative.

The file sat on my disk for five weeks. Today I finally had the time.

## The raw recording

Over half an hour of mushroom is slow listening. I sped it up six and a half times: same notes, same pitch. Then I played it through a software synth with some reverb. Six minutes of fly agaric:
